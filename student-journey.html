Formalising supervision — the MOU

Steps

  • Contact the academic linked to your topic and confirm availability and feasibility.
  • Agree the question, scope, timelines and expectations for feedback.
  • Complete the Memorandum of Understanding in PeopleSoft. You complete it; the supervisor approves; it routes to the HOD for final approval.

Clinical platforms

MMed projects are often anchored at one of the Department's teaching hospitals, each with its own case mix and site-approval route (see Stage 6): Groote Schuur, Red Cross War Memorial Children's Hospital, Mowbray Maternity, New Somerset Hospital, and rural outreach sites.

For degree-related research, the primary supervisor is listed as Principal Investigator on faculty forms. The PI and the Head of Department may not be the same person.
Stage 3

Synopsis FHS014 and protocol FHS015

The synopsis is a concise, maximum four-page summary used for early departmental review. The protocol expands it into the full study plan.

Synopsis structure — FHS014
  1. Research question — stated clearly and concisely.
  2. Background and rationale — why the topic matters; the gap addressed.
  3. Aims and objectives — what the study intends to answer.
  4. Methods — design, setting, population, data source, analysis plan.
  5. Ethical considerations — risks, consent, confidentiality, vulnerable groups.
  6. Anticipated significance — contribution to care, service or knowledge.

Guide: FHS014hlp

Protocol structure — FHS015
  1. Study design and setting — justify the design for the question.
  2. Population, sampling and recruitment — eligibility, sample size, recruitment.
  3. Data collection — sources, variables, instruments, quality assurance.
  4. Data management and analysis plan — capture, storage, cleaning, statistics.
  5. Ethics, consent and governance — consent, confidentiality, permissions.
  6. Budget and timeline — realistic milestones through to submission.

Guide: FHS015hlp · Faculty forms: FHS forms library

Stage 4

Departmental Research Committee review

The Anaesthesia and Perioperative Medicine DRC supports and promotes research and training productivity at all levels — monitoring, guiding and facilitating departmental research activity, and ensuring alignment with Faculty priorities, procedures and ethics requirements — and signs off proposals before HREC submission. Committee members have an interest and track record in research and are available to advise on funding, resources, regulatory process and supervision. TO CONFIRM current committee membership and chair — the Department's DRC page notes that full details are still being published.

Statistical support. Confirm with your supervisor or the DRC whether a statistical-consultation meeting is required at protocol-development stage for your project — this is a firm requirement in some FHS departments but has not been confirmed as departmental policy for Anaesthesia. The FHS-wide statistical support and Research Methods Course options under Tools & Training are open to you regardless.
The postgraduate approval route — five steps
  1. Prepare the proposal according to your division's guidelines — your supervisor provides these.
  2. Independent scientific review — your supervisor arranges review by two independent experts in the field (they need not be UCT staff). Your supervisor and co-supervisors may not act as reviewers.
  3. If your division runs postgraduate seminars, presenting there may satisfy this review, with sign-off from two academics not directly involved in your project.
  4. Complete the Departmental Research Proposal Checklist (DRPC) and collect signatures from the two reviewers, your supervisor, and co-investigators.
  5. DRC and HOD approval on the signed DRPC — then submit the approved proposal with it for ethics review, and send the signed proposal plus the FHS PGO forms to the FHS Postgraduate Office for faculty approval.
Submission requirements and forms
  • Every new protocol submitted for DRC/HOD signature must include the online Protocol Submission Tracking form together with the FHS013 (form · guide).
  • Postgraduate submissions additionally require the signed DRPC.

Research involving human participants, human data or research animals also requires HREC or RAEC review — see Stage 5.

Stage 5

Faculty HREC ethics approval

Every protocol involving human participants, human biological material or human-derived data must have HREC approval before the study begins. Retrospective folder reviews and database studies still require DRC review and the appropriate HREC process; scoping and systematic reviews require a waiver or exemption from the HREC Chair.

Preparing and submitting the application

Compile one combined PDF: DRC-signed FHS013 · synopsis · protocol · appendices · participant information sheet and consent forms (if applicable) · cover letter with the thesis title and document list.

  • Submit via the HREC online submission system.
  • Expedited review is available for lower-risk studies (e.g. retrospective folder reviews); full committee review applies to higher-risk protocols.
  • Approval is typically valid for one year and must be renewed annually until the study closes.

Stage 7

Thesis submission and examination

Two stages in PeopleSoft: lodge the Intention to Submit, then upload the thesis for examination. Path: Self Service → Research Activities → Service Requests → Create New Request → Thesis / Dissertation related requests.

Stage A — Intention to submit D8 (≥ 6 weeks before)

Form D8 must be lodged at least six weeks before the intended submission date. Upload with it:

  • D8 Intention to Submit — signed by trainee and supervisor.
  • D15 Intellectual Property assessment.
  • Updated abstract as PDF — named Abstract-open.pdf (or Abstract-Restricted.pdf if neutralised).
  • Valid HREC approval letter (renewed if expired).
  • D16 Open-access suppression — only if an embargo applies.

The Faculty Office then asks the supervisor to nominate examiners and complete supervisor forms.

Stage B — Upload the thesis D18 D19
  • Thesis.pdf — saved exactly as Thesis.pdf, with the D19 plagiarism declaration bound inside, immediately after the title page.
  • D18 Declaration / word count — uploaded as a separate PDF.
  • Final or updated abstract (same naming convention).
  • Title-change form if the final title differs from the approved title.

Type in the comment box: “I confirm that the uploaded document is the thesis/dissertation to be examined.”

Deadlines, fees and Turnitin
  • As a guide: submit by 15 March for June graduation and 15 August for December graduation — always confirm with the Faculty Office.
  • Submitting before the start of the academic year avoids fees; before mid-July may qualify for a 50% rebate.
  • Run a Turnitin similarity check on the draft via your Faculty Amathuba site and review it with your supervisor — the report itself is not uploaded with the thesis.
Tools & training

Ethics training, referencing and data tools

Research ethics training — mandatory

Required for all researchers and students in health research. From 1 July 2026 HREC will not process applications or progress reports without verified certification. Renew every 3 years.

  • UCT-preferred: ORI Modules 4 & 5 (~3 h each) via SuccessFactors.
  • Accepted alternatives (assessed + certified): TRREE 1–3, TCPS 2 CORE, OHRP, WHO, CITI.
  • Clinical trials additionally require valid SA GCP certification.

Referencing & similarity

  • EndNote — free for UCT staff and students via ICTS; style guidance from UCT Libraries.
  • Turnitin — check drafts via your Faculty Amathuba site before submission.

REDCap

UCT-hosted secure data capture for case report forms, surveys and study databases — appropriate for POPIA-compliant storage. Training and access.

Statistics & research methods

  • Statistical support — ask your supervisor or the DRC whether a departmental or DRC-funded consultation service is available for Anaesthesia postgraduates; several FHS departments run one, but this has not been confirmed for Anaesthesia specifically. TO CONFIRM

  • FHS Research Methods Course — see the dedicated panel below for the 2026 module dates and registration. Other offerings appear on Amathuba, UCT’s learning platform, and via departmental comms.
  • Self-paced: Coursera Understanding Clinical Research, Clinical Epidemiology, Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis.

FHS Research Methods Course — 2026

Designed for MMed students but open to all FHS postgraduates (MPhil, MSc, PhD). Four modules, each a Friday morning 09:00–12:00 (coffee/tea from 08:30); the full 4-module course runs three times a year, and modules can be attended whenever convenient.

  • Modules 1–2 (Foundations) — for early registrars in the first 6–12 months of training, before DRC and HREC protocol submission (later attendance fine if the dissertation hasn’t started, or as a refresher).
  • Modules 3–4 (Finalisation) — for registrars who have collected their data, regardless of year of study.

2026 dates — Course 1: 6, 13, 20, 27 Mar · Course 2: 5, 15, 22, 29 May · Course 3: 14, 21, 28 Aug & 4 Sep.

Funding

Funding and publication support

Departmental registrar research funding

Internal support for dissertation costs (research assistance, data capture, statistics) should be discussed on an individual basis with your supervisor.

Conference travel

Presenting MMed work (oral or poster) → apply for the URC Conference Travel Grant via the eRA portal. Attendance without presenting is not funded.

Open access — OAJPF

Up to R20 000 toward APCs for accepted articles in DHET-accredited open-access journals. Apply only after acceptance; where the student is corresponding author, the supervisor applies. Claims within 6 months of invoice. Details.

Page cost grants

Contribution toward page charges in DHET-accredited journals, via eRA. Verify accreditation with the DHET Journal Finder; negotiate developing-country discounts first.

External funding — NRF, SAMRC and beyond
  • NRF · SAMRC · Wellcome · NIH · professional societies.
  • Find calls via Pivot-RP, the UCT Research Announcements newsletter, and the FHS Research Noticeboard.
  • Obtain institutional clearance through the UCT Research Office before applying, and align budgets with the departmental finance team.
